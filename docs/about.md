# About

The Restructuring Brief is a weekly summary of what happened in restructuring
and insolvency, written for people who work in it and people who are learning
it, in the same seven minutes.

[Subscribe](subscribe.md){ .md-button .md-button--primary }

## Scope

The newsletter covers two things, and they have **deliberately different
geographic scopes**. This is the most important thing to understand about it.

**Restructurings: worldwide.** The largest restructurings anywhere — a Chapter
11 in Texas, an English scheme used by a Swiss group, a *recuperação
extrajudicial* in Belo Horizonte, a StaRUG in Frankfurt. What matters is the
size and the structure, not the country. A matter qualifies if it involves
roughly $500m or more of debt, a listed parent at any size, or proceedings in
more than one jurisdiction.

**Case law: the United Kingdom and the United States only.** Not because
nothing interesting happens elsewhere, but because these are the two systems
this newsletter can analyse properly rather than merely report. A compressed
summary of a German or Dutch judgment, written from a law-firm note by someone
who has not read the original in the original, is worse than no coverage at
all.

So you will sometimes see a Brazilian filing in the situations section and no
Brazilian case note. That is a choice, not an oversight.

## What is in an issue

| Section | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **The week in three lines** | The whole issue, if that is all you read |
| **Situation of the week** | One restructuring explained properly, in about 250 words |
| **Situations** | The other significant matters, grouped by stage: filed, in negotiation, completed |
| **Case notes** | UK and US decisions. Bottom line, facts, question, holding, why it matters, and background for anyone new to the doctrine |
| **Both sides of the table** | One legal concept and one financial one, usually the same idea seen from the two seats |
| **Conditions** | Credit spreads and rates, and what they imply for the filings ahead |
| **Watchlist** | Hearings, appeals and decisions worth waiting for |

## Who it is for

Two audiences at once, which shapes how every item is written.

**Practitioners** — lawyers, advisers, insolvency practitioners, investors —
get the point first. Every case note opens with a bottom line of two or three
lines. Reading only those takes about two minutes.

**Students** get the same items with the doctrine explained underneath. Each
case note carries a background paragraph, and every issue explains two
concepts from first principles. Those collect in the
[glossary](glossary.md), which after a year is a hundred terms, half law and
half finance.

## How it is made

Developments are gathered each week from public sources: SEC EDGAR filings,
CourtListener, the National Archives' Find Case Law service, the Insolvency
Service, the Federal Reserve Bank of St Louis for market data, and commentary
from law firms and advisers.

Two rules govern the sourcing, and they are enforced by the software rather
than left to good intentions.

**Every case note links to the judgment itself.** Commentary is used to find
decisions and to sanity check a reading of one. It is never the cited
authority for what a court held. An issue will not build if a case note has no
primary source.

**Nothing behind a paywall is cited.** Students cannot open it, so it does not
belong here.

A draft is assembled with the help of software and then read and edited by a
person before anything is published. Summaries of judgments are compressed by
necessity, and compression loses things; the link to the primary source is
there for that reason.

## What it is not

This newsletter is published for information and education only. It is not
legal, financial or investment advice. It is not a substitute for advice on any
specific matter, and nothing in it creates a professional relationship of any
kind. Do not act on a summary. Read the decision, and take advice.

## Corrections

Corrections matter more here than speed. If something is wrong, please say so
by [opening an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/gattusopierre01-cloud/The-Restructuring-Newsletter/issues).
Corrections are made on the website and noted in the next edition.

## Privacy

If you subscribe, your email address is held by the mail provider for the
purpose of sending you the newsletter and nothing else. It is not sold, shared
or used for anything other than this publication. Every issue carries an
unsubscribe link, and unsubscribing deletes the address.

## Colophon

Built in the open. The site, the PDF and the email are generated from a single
file per issue; the code is
[on GitHub](https://github.com/gattusopierre01-cloud/The-Restructuring-Newsletter).
The PDF is typeset with Typst in Libertinus Serif. The crest divides *per
pale*: scales on one side, a waterfall of bars on the other, for the two sides
of the table.
